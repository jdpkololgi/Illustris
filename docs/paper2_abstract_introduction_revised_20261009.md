## Redshift scope: important clarification

The provisional VAC covers the selected BGS sample over **0.15 <= z < 0.55**, not just 0.20 <= z < 0.30. Its 5,436,413 rows include 5,404,568 supported objects and 31,845 null/flagged objects. The CIGALE analysis has 4,989,187 usable supported CG15 galaxies across the catalogue; 2,327,331 lie in the full-footprint 0.20 <= z < 0.30 subset. The latter is the parent sample for the quoted controlled property comparison, not necessarily the final number retained in every common-cell estimator.

“Full footprint” describes angular coverage, not redshift coverage. The draft retains the full-range catalogue as the deliverable and explicitly labels the narrower numerical example. This does not certify that low-redshift results are free of transfer or SED systematics. No full-range aggregate effect has been substituted for the documented slice result.

## Abstract — Overleaf-ready fragment

```latex
\begin{abstract}
Large spectroscopic surveys enable statistical studies of galaxy properties across the cosmic web, but their sparse and incomplete galaxy samples do not uniquely determine the underlying matter field. We treat environmental inference as a probabilistic inverse problem and present an amortised simulation-based inference pipeline for the Dark Energy Spectroscopic Instrument Bright Galaxy Survey (DESI BGS). Using BGS-like catalogues derived from AbacusSummit, we combine a frozen residual U-Net compressor with a conditional flow-matching posterior estimator trained on out-of-fold summaries. The target is the joint distribution of three tidal eigenvalues at a smoothing scale of $R_s=7\,h^{-1}\,\mathrm{Mpc}$ under the adopted mock and survey-response model. Posterior validation uses held-out simulations and diagnostics of coverage and survey-response dependence. We construct a provisional value-added catalogue containing 5,436,413 selected galaxies over $0.15\leq z<0.55$, with posterior products for supported objects and explicit quality flags. We demonstrate its use through posterior-weighted comparisons of CIGALE-derived galaxy properties. Across the survey footprint at $0.20\leq z<0.30$, standardising in stellar mass and redshift gives a knot-minus-void difference of approximately $-0.186$ dex in mean logarithmic specific star-formation rate and $+7.13$ percentage points in the low-specific-star-formation-rate fraction. These are descriptive associations with inferred environments, not causal effects. The catalogue retains continuous environmental information and supports probabilistic classification, while its observational interpretation remains conditional on the adopted model and limited by mock--data mismatch and property-catalogue selection.
\end{abstract}

\begin{keywords}
large-scale structure of Universe -- methods: statistical -- galaxies: evolution
\end{keywords}
```

## Introduction — Overleaf-ready fragment

```latex
\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:introduction}

The relationship between galaxy properties and their surroundings is a central observational constraint on galaxy formation and evolution. Large spectroscopic surveys make it possible to study these associations across the connected network of voids, sheets, filaments and dense nodes that constitutes the cosmic web. Earlier observational studies have established a substantial basis for this programme, while demonstrating the importance of distinguishing web geometry from correlated quantities such as stellar mass and local density \citep{eardley2015_gama_web,kraljic2018_galaxy_web}. The Dark Energy Spectroscopic Instrument Bright Galaxy Survey (DESI BGS) extends the statistical reach of such studies. Exploiting this opportunity requires an environmental description whose interpretation accounts for the fact that observed galaxies are biased, sparse and incompletely selected tracers of the underlying matter distribution.
% TODO: Add the appropriate DESI instrument, BGS survey and exact catalogue-release references.

Galaxy environment is not a uniquely defined observable. Local galaxy density, group membership and distance from a filament describe different aspects of a galaxy's surroundings and need not provide equivalent classifications. Here we adopt the T-web framework, which characterises environment through the eigenvalues of the tidal tensor, defined as the Hessian of a smoothed gravitational potential \citep{forero-romeroDynamicalClassificationCosmic2009}. The number of eigenvalues exceeding a chosen threshold determines the associated void, sheet, filament or knot classification. Both the smoothing scale and the threshold enter this operational definition. Modelling the three eigenvalues jointly retains information that is discarded by a categorical label and allows environmental probabilities to be derived subsequently for different thresholds. In this usage, a T-web knot denotes a tidal classification and does not necessarily identify a gravitationally bound galaxy cluster.

Inferring these quantities from a survey is a probabilistic inverse problem. A finite galaxy catalogue does not uniquely specify the matter distribution: distinct latent fields can remain compatible with the same observed tracer configuration and can imply different tidal environments. Uncertainty therefore need not disappear even when a predictive model has been trained successfully. The relevant target is a conditional distribution of tidal eigenvalues, rather than a single best environmental assignment. This distribution weights latent possibilities according to the adopted cosmological, galaxy--halo and observational model; it is not a model-independent enumeration of all possible environments. When inference uses a learned compression of the observations, its interpretation additionally depends on the information retained by that representation. Posterior width is consequently meaningful only alongside evidence that the inferred distribution adequately represents uncertainty under the stated modelling assumptions.

Existing approaches address complementary versions of this reconstruction problem. Topological methods such as DisPerSE extract web structure from a density representation constructed from discrete tracers, without requiring direct observations of the underlying dark-matter density field \citep{sousbie2011_persistent_web}. Bayesian field-reconstruction methods such as BORG instead infer data-constrained field realisations within a forward model \citep{jascheBayesianPhysicalReconstruction2013}; these can be used to obtain probabilistic tidal-web classifications \citep{leclercq2015_bayesian_web}. Probabilistic environmental catalogues have also been constructed for DESI using ASTRA \citep{zapata-zuluaga_cosmic_2026}. More recently, \citet{thiele2026_graph_flows} developed graph-based flow matching for stochastic void-catalogue inference from galaxy tracers. These methods differ in their inferred objects---fields, structural catalogues or environmental memberships---and in the uncertainty represented by their outputs. Our objective is direct amortised inference of continuous, local tidal-eigenvalue distributions, rather than a globally coherent posterior over the matter field or a catalogue of geometric web structures.

In Paper~I, \citet{kololgi_learning_2026} demonstrated that graph-based models could use the geometry of an IllustrisTNG-300 galaxy configuration to predict discrete T-web environments. That study established the utility of configuration-level information in an idealised simulation setting. Moving to observational inference requires more than replacing a categorical output with a continuous point estimate. Hard classifications discard within-class variation, and uncertainty can extend across multiple environmental classes rather than being confined to objects immediately adjacent to a classification boundary. A survey application must also account for changes in tracer sampling and observing conditions, and test whether its uncertainty estimates remain informative on simulations not used to fit or select the model. The present work therefore shifts the emphasis from classification performance alone to the interpretation and validation of conditional environmental distributions.

Amortised simulation-based inference provides a practical framework for this task by learning an observation-conditioned distribution from simulated examples \citep{cranmerFrontierSimulationbasedInference2020,dax_flow_2023}. We use BGS-like mock catalogues derived from AbacusSummit \citep{maksimova2021_abacussummit} and the observational effects represented in their survey-response construction. Our pipeline combines a residual U-Net compressor of the observed galaxy configuration with a separate conditional flow-matching posterior estimator. Out-of-fold summaries are generated using compressors that exclude the corresponding simulation phase from training, so that the posterior estimator learns from held-out compression behaviour rather than exclusively from in-sample predictions. The deployed compressor is frozen. We distinguish training, model selection and independent mock evaluation, and assess posterior behaviour as a function of redshift and survey response rather than relying solely on pooled accuracy or coverage.

The inferred quantity is the joint distribution of the ordered eigenvalues at an individual galaxy location, at $R_s=7\,h^{-1}\,\mathrm{Mpc}$ and under the fixed-$z=0.2$ target convention of the adopted mocks. Here, ``joint'' refers to dependence among the three eigenvalues, not to dependence among different galaxy locations. Independently drawing from these local distributions does not produce a coherent matter-field realisation. We derive T-web membership probabilities from posterior samples, adopting $\lambda_{\rm th}=0.2$ for the primary environmental comparison; the classification threshold is not imposed as the training target. The fixed-snapshot convention also means that trends with observed redshift cannot, by themselves, be interpreted as physical evolution of the tidal field.

We apply the pipeline to the selected DESI BGS Loa catalogue over $0.15\leq z<0.55$, retaining the full selected redshift range in a provisional value-added catalogue with explicit support and quality flags. As a scientific application, we cross-match this product to CIGALE-derived stellar masses and star-formation rates and compare galaxy-property distributions using posterior environmental probabilities instead of only hard labels. We examine the selected matched sample across the catalogue's redshift range and report a mass- and redshift-standardised comparison over $0.20\leq z<0.30$ as a clearly specified example, not as a restriction of the VAC to that interval. These comparisons measure descriptive associations with inferred environments. Posterior weighting does not, without additional assumptions, establish either a causal environmental effect or unbiased recovery of the property distribution in each latent true class.

Mock-based validation and observational deployment are distinct parts of this programme. The true tidal eigenvalues of the observed galaxies are unavailable, so passing coverage tests on simulations does not establish coverage on DESI. Known differences between mock and observed tracer populations, particularly towards the high-redshift end of the catalogue, limit transfer of the inferred distributions. Matching and quality requirements in the property catalogue introduce further redshift- and environment-dependent selection. We therefore present the observational product and its galaxy-property associations together with these limitations, rather than treating full-range deployment as evidence of uniform scientific validity. The central contribution is the combination of continuous environmental inference, out-of-fold neural compression, explicit posterior validation and a survey-scale application with stated conditions of interpretation.
```

## Editorial checks before submission

- **Full range versus illustrative result:** the catalogue extends to 0.55, and the broader property analysis exists. The quoted -0.186 dex/+7.13 percentage-point result is specifically for 0.20 <= z < 0.30. If a full-range headline is preferred, calculate and document that exact estimator; do not relabel this result. Consider presenting redshift-resolved curves rather than one aggregate that hides changing selection.
- **Catalogue naming:** the manuscript uses “DESI BGS Loa” rather than asserting an unverified mapping between data assembly, spectroscopic release and LSS release. Once the exact provenance is confirmed, consistently insert the intended DR2 designation, LSS version, magnitude selection, footprint and citations. It is not a statement that your intended release is unavailable or incorrect.
- **Calibration sentence:** the abstract deliberately gives no invented TARP score. Add one concise quantitative held-out-mock result after selecting the precise checkpoint and nominal coverage levels to report. Distinguish the original blinded result from the halo-48 deployment refit's checks. Avoid implying exact conditional calibration or real-data coverage.
- **Response modelling:** enumerate actual mock components in Methods, including the precise role of flux selection, redshift-space distortions, randoms, boundaries, fibre assignment/altMTL and redshift failures. The draft does not assert that every proposed component was implemented.
- **Property result definition:** define low sSFR as log10(sSFR/yr^-1) < -11, confirm the new product uses this same threshold, and specify the common mass/redshift cells and weighting. CG15 uses five Tractor and ten spectrophotometric bands. Keep the 2,327,331 slice parent count distinct from estimator-specific retained rows.
- **Uncertainties:** the log gives spatial-bootstrap 16th–84th percentile intervals [-0.189,-0.184] dex and [7.03,7.22] percentage points for the CG15 slice contrasts. These do not include all SED or simulation-to-observation systematics and should not be presented as a complete error budget. The abstract omits them pending the final reporting choice.
- **High-redshift selection:** unique-match selection retains only about 46–52% of supported environmental probability weight at 0.50–0.55, versus about 97% at 0.20–0.30. This is separate from the mock–data mismatch and must remain visible in full-range property figures. Neither limitation justifies silently deleting the full-range catalogue from the paper.
- **Scope:** the conditional-field project is not described as a validated result of this local-posterior pipeline. A later discussion paragraph can introduce it as an extension, unless the paper's final scope and evidence explicitly incorporate it.
- **Style:** reconcile keywords with the target journal's accepted list. Add the final section roadmap once section numbering is fixed. Citation keys below are suggestions, not a claim that they exist in your Overleaf bibliography.
